The unique kernel

A reason to change your mind.

A visitor encounters a compelling opposing argument—and changes their position.

That moment demonstrates the central value: exploring perspectives and thinking beyond an initial preference.

Who walks in?

Curious. Opinionated. Open to another angle.

A student, developer, or technology enthusiast arrives through an interesting comparison shared by a friend, social media, or the discovery view.

Today, those debates are scattered across threads, forums, and comments. Here, both sides and their reasoning are easy to examine without registering.

The idea, connected

Curiosity → participation → reconsideration

Read either side before participating. After choosing, see the position distribution, examine arguments, mark useful reasoning, and contribute. Discovering new perspectives gives visitors a reason to return.

The one-minute demo

“Which is better suited to new systems programming projects?”

  1. Discover the Rust vs. C++ card, with a featured argument and participant count.
  2. Open the comparison, read either side, and choose a position.
  3. See results, mark an argument helpful, and add a short argument.
  4. Encounter an opposing argument and change position. Watch the choice and results update.
What makes it convincing

Real interactions. Simple persistence.

Refresh or return in the same browser: the selected position, helpful marks, and contributed arguments are restored automatically using localStorage.

Changing position updates the existing selection. Helpful marks toggle to prevent duplicate endorsements. Community figures are seeded demonstration data, combined with the visitor’s local selection.

Finish line: responsive, distinctive, reliable; demonstrable in a minute. Target 2–4 hours of active work, a short demo video, and a public GitHub repository. Deployment is optional.

Keep the experiment small

The scope boundary

Now · prove the complete loop
  • One seeded Rust vs. C++ comparison, a discovery card, and sample arguments.
  • Read both sides; select Rust, C++, or Undecided; view the demonstration distribution.
  • Toggle helpful marks, contribute short arguments, and change position.
  • Persist and restore interaction state locally in the same browser.
  • A responsive, visually distinctive experience with clearly labeled demo figures.
Later · expand discovery and participation
  • Create and publish comparisons.
  • A broader discovery feed.
  • Everyday choices, science, education, culture, business, public issues, and other subjects beyond the seeded technology comparison.
Cut from this experiment · keep the infrastructure small
  • Authentication and real accounts: registration is unnecessary to prove this loop.
  • Databases, shared backend, and live multiplayer: local state is sufficient for the demonstration.
  • Browser and device synchronization: same-browser persistence keeps implementation reliable and simple.
